# F1 — Anti-slop is specificity, not retro styling

AI-slop criticism consistently points to sameness and interchangeable product identity. The remedy for dnPeople is not “make it old”; it is to make every important surface say something specific about HR operations: payroll review, branch exceptions, attendance evidence, approvals, and policy state.

Evidence: Built In identifies generic gradients, blobs, repeated card layouts, and interchangeable flows as the recognizable pattern. The arXiv study adds a broader warning about superficial competence and shifted review burden. These support the hypothesis that dnPeople needs more authored workflow evidence, not more decorative novelty.

Design implication: replace generic dashboard/marketing decoration with one recurring dnPeople motif—“record → status → reviewer → evidence”—across landing, tables, and detail panels.
