# Source 01 — Built In: The New Skeuomorphism

- URL: https://builtin.com/articles/ai-design-slop-era
- Type: industry commentary
- Retrieved: 2026-09-26
- Credibility: medium; useful for current design discourse, not a controlled usability study.

## Relevant evidence

The article defines AI design slop as generic, interchangeable software experiences that pass technical review while lacking a specific brand identity. It identifies repeated gradient blobs, abstract hero illustrations, generic dashboard cards, and interchangeable onboarding flows as recognizable symptoms.

Short quote: “It doesn’t look like something specific. It looks like everything, approximately.”

The article also makes an important distinction for the dnPeople decision: old skeuomorphism at least had an identifiable point of view and attempted to solve an affordance problem. The lesson is not to copy old decoration, but to make visual decisions that communicate a real product point of view.

## Use in decision

Use as a heuristic for removing generic composition patterns from dnPeople. Do not use it as proof that dark themes, gradients, or rounded cards are inherently bad.
