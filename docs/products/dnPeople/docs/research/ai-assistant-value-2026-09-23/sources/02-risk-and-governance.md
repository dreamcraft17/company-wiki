# Source notes — risk and governance

## NIST AI RMF

NIST frames trustworthy AI work as govern, map, measure, and manage. For dnPeople this translates to a documented owner, allowed data/tools, evaluation set, incident path, and kill switch for model synthesis.

Source: [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)

## Employment-related AI risk

The EU AI Act identifies AI used in employment and worker management—such as recruitment, promotion, termination, task allocation, monitoring, or evaluation—as a sensitive/high-risk area. This is not an automatic legal classification for every dnPeople feature, but it is a strong product boundary: assistant v1 should answer and guide, not score people or recommend employment decisions.

Source: [European Commission AI Act Service Desk — Recital 57](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/recital-57)

## Adoption research

Recent HR research points to adoption being shaped by language, role, content quality, training, and guidance—not just model capability. This supports an Indonesian-language, policy-grounded assistant with visible sources and onboarding rather than a generic chat box.

Sources: [AIHR HR Priorities 2026](https://www.aihr.com/resources/AIHR_HR_Priorities_2026_Report.pdf), [AI adoption in HR organisations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17887)
